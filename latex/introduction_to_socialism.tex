\section{Study Quesions}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item What are some examples you can think of that show the alienation and division within the working class? What are some counter-examples?
	\item Why is the working class "the one class in society with a material interest in challenging all forms of oppression"?
	\item How does the working class get divided under capitalism? What stands in the way of the members of the working class recognizing their common interests and uniting?
	\item How are systems of oppression, such as white supremacy and patriarchy, related to the system of exploitation (capitalism)?
	\item What is the author's critique of privilege theory?
	\item Why do some members of the working class accept reactionary ideas, even if these ideas contradict their material interests? How can this dynamic be overcome?
	\item What is class struggle, and how does participating in the struggle lead to greater consciousness by the working class?
	\item Why is revolutionary organization so important for the class struggle?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Study Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item What's the social democratic view of the relationship between party and class?
	\item How were Rosa Luxemburg's ideas different from the ideas of social democrats like Kautsky? How does she relate the "conservative" role a party can play to the "spontaneity" of the masses?
	\item How are Trotsky's ideas similar to Luxemburg's? What are his arguments in support of his distrust of centralized organization?
	\item How do Lenin and Gramsci's thought acknowledge the problems identified by Luxemburg and Trotsky but also move beyond them? What is the role of the party in relation to the working class?
	\item How is the Bolshevik (Leninist) conception of the party distinct from the social democratic and Stalinist conceptions?
	\item Why is it undesirable for the Leninist party to become the state after capitalism is overthrown? What is the role of the party to the masses during revolution?
\end{enumerate}
